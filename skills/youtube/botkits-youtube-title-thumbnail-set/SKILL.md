# YouTube title and thumbnail set

Pick the title and thumbnail for one video as a pair. The title and the thumbnail share one job: make the right viewer curious enough to click, then let the video keep that promise.

## 1. Ask for the inputs

| Input | Default | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Topic, one sentence | none, ask | What the video is about |
| The one thing the viewer gets | none, ask | A fact, a result, a method or a story. If the user cannot say it, stop and ask for it. |
| Viewer | none, ask | Who clicks and what they already know |
| Format | long video | Or Short |
| Real proof available | none | A number, a clip, an object or a face that is really in the video |
| Words to avoid | none | Brand rules, banned claims |

If the video exists, read its script or transcript and take the promise from there. The promise is the thing that happens in the first minute, not the one you wish happened.

## 2. Write the promise line

Write one line: "After this video the viewer knows or can do ___." Every title and thumbnail below must be true to that line. If you change the line, say so.

## 3. Write 10 titles

Write 10 titles. Aim for about 60 characters or fewer, because mobile and search often cut longer ones. Check the cut in the user's own Studio preview, not by assumption.

Spread them over these shapes so the 10 are not one idea in 10 coats:

1. Question the viewer already asks.
2. Plain statement of the result.
3. Number or amount that is really in the video.
4. Contrast between two things.
5. A mistake or myth the video corrects.
6. A small test or experiment.
7. "How" with a named outcome.
8. A time or cost limit that is true.
9. A name or object that is the star of the video.
10. Your own pick.

Rules for every title:

- True. The video shows it. Strike any title that promises something later than the first third of the video.
- Specific. One concrete noun or number beats a general word.
- No claim about anyone's results that you cannot open and cite.
- No all caps words, no stacked punctuation, no emoji unless the channel already uses them.

## 4. Write 3 thumbnail concepts

Write 3 concepts, each different in its main picture. For each one give:

- **Picture.** One subject and one background. Describe what is visible in a frame of the video, or say it needs a new shot.
- **Words.** Three words or fewer, big enough to read on a phone. Zero words is allowed.
- **Contrast.** What is light and what is dark, and where the eye lands first.
- **Adds to the title.** One line that says what the thumbnail shows that the title does not say. If it only repeats the title, rewrite it.

Rules: one face or one object as the focus. No tiny type. No arrows or circles unless the video is about the thing they point at. Keep important parts away from the corner where the duration badge sits.

## 5. Score and rank

Score each title and each concept from 0 to 2 on these six checks. Show the table.

| Check | 2 | 0 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| True to the promise line | Yes, in the first third | No |
| Names the viewer's problem or wish | Clearly | Not at all |
| Specific noun or number | Yes | Only general words |
| Reads at a glance | Under 3 seconds | Needs a second read |
| Title and thumbnail add to each other | Yes | They repeat or clash |
| Would the user say it out loud | Yes | Sounds like a trick |

Top two titles and top concept go forward. If two options tie, pick the plainer one.

## 6. Run the phone-size check

Ask the user to look at the thumbnail at about the size of a thumb on a phone, next to the title. Then answer in one line each:

1. Can you read the words?
2. Can you tell what the video is about without the title?
3. Does the picture still work in black and white?

If any answer is no, change the concept, not the checks.

## 7. Return

Return, in this order:

1. The promise line.
2. The ranked titles, best first, with scores.
3. The ranked thumbnail concepts with scores.
4. The pair you suggest, and one line on why.
5. One spare pair to swap in after a few days if the first pair's click rate sits below the channel's usual, read from YouTube Studio. Name the metric. Do not invent a target.

## Limits

- It does not make the image. Hand a chosen concept to an image tool or a designer.
- It does not read live YouTube data. Click rates and results come from the user's Studio.
- A good pair does not rescue a video that breaks its own promise.
